Our Story

Built One Room
at a Time

Shivansh Interiors began in 2012, working out of a single rented room in Surat, with one designer, a drafting table and a conviction that good design shouldn't be reserved for the very few. The first project was a 400 sq.ft. apartment — the client couldn't afford to redo anything twice, so I didn't let her.

That discipline never left. Fourteen years and 180 projects later, I still treat a small apartment with the same rigour as an eight-bedroom villa — because the client on the other side of the brief deserves nothing less.

Mission & Vision

What We're
Building Toward

Mission — To design interiors that improve the way our clients live, work and gather, through honest materials, disciplined planning and craftsmanship that holds up under daily life, not just on shoot day.

Vision — To be the studio Indian families and businesses trust first when a space matters — recognised not for scale, but for the consistency of the work, project after project.

Design Philosophy

Restraint is
a Material Too

We design from the outside in — light first, then material, then form. A room is drawn many times before a single finish is chosen, because the wrong layout can't be fixed by the right sofa.

Every project carries one dominant material and a handful of quiet supporting ones. The result should feel edited, not accumulated — a space with nothing left to remove.

The Studio, Not the Staff

It's Just Me —
On Purpose

Shivansh Interiors has never hired past its founder. Every plan is drawn by the same hand, every material chosen by the same eye, every site walked by the same person who took your first call. That's not a limitation I've settled for — it's the whole model.

Vishal Kamboya — Founder & Principal Designer. Fourteen years, 180 projects, one point of contact from the first sketch to the final walkthrough. I work with a trusted, hand-picked network of architects, contractors and craftspeople on every project — but the design decisions, and the accountability for them, stay with me.
